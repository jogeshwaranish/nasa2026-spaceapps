Back-casting VIIRS from MODIS —

A model is trained on 2012 onward, when both sensors were flying, to predict what VIIRS (375 m) would have detected from what MODIS (1 km) detected. It is then applied to 2000–2011, before VIIRS existed.

How well does it work on years it hasn't seen?

Leave-one-year-out: each year 2012–2026 is held out, the model is trained on the others, and scored on the held-out year. “Typical error” is the typical multiplicative miss (1.20× = within about 20%).

Limits

  • The predictions contain no new observations: they re-express MODIS on VIIRS's scale. They cannot recover small fires MODIS missed entirely.
  • The training years can't tell us whether the MODIS–VIIRS relationship was the same in 2000–2011.
  • 2000 to mid-2002 is Terra only; the model learned from Terra + Aqua, so those years are the least reliable.
  • Data is 5 sampled days per month (13th–17th), so each month is noisy. Fetching the full record would tighten everything.
